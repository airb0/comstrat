# Principles, tone of voice, visual principle

The layer that turns the platform into behaviour and copy. All three come from the archetype pair and the value proposition — never written in isolation.

## Principles

Four to six. Each is a verb phrase plus one or two sentences on how the brand behaves because of it. A principle is something the brand could break — if nobody could ever do the opposite, it's a platitude.

Example — a national car maker:
- **Bring benefit** — everything we do must bring tangible benefit not only to us but to clients, partners, society, the country and the world.
- **Be free of stereotypes** — we see opportunities where others don't look, so we offer better solutions, not just familiar ones.
- **Act decisively** — no empty talk; we decide fast and move confidently toward the goal.
- **Don't stop at what's achieved** — we keep learning to inspire change across whole industries.
- **Keep our word** — if we promise, it will be so. Full stop.

Check: each principle traces to the leading or complementary archetype, or to the Why.

## Tone of voice

Three to five "In communication we strive to…" rules. Each has a **✔ / ✖ pair** — a real line that does it and one that doesn't, in the client's category. Typical rules:

- Talk about what matters to the customer, not about our achievements. ✔ *Trust a reliable car maker* ✖ *We are the largest car company in the country*
- Be energetic and inspiring. ✔ *Your pickup. Your rules* ✖ *The pickup offers four-wheel drive, high cross-country ability and a roomy bed*
- Be simple, brief, clear. ✔ *A new sedan from 20 a month* ✖ *The sedan on credit for 36 months, 20% down at 13.9% a year*
- Headlines (large text) speak of the new possibility the offer gives, not its details. ✔ *With this clearance, potholes don't exist* ✖ *Ground clearance 195 mm*
- Body copy (small text) states facts, not emotions or judgements. ✔ *Ground clearance 195 mm* ✖ *High clearance*

The rules must be in the archetype pair's voice (here: Explorer energy, Regular Guy plainness). Write the pairs in the client's language.

## Visual principle

One or two sentences that tie the image to the platform's meaning, plus reference pictures. Example — the car maker: dynamic images with open perspective and clear perspective lines, to stress moving forward toward the goal. Not a style guide — a single rule a photographer or AI prompt can follow. The actual look (fonts, colours, logo) comes from the client's brand book or site.
