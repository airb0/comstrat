# Brand archetype — leading + complementary

**The archetype belongs to the brand, not the product.** A product, channel or campaign speaks in its brand's archetype. If a product genuinely has an archetype of its own, it is a sub-brand — say so and treat it as a brand. In a product deck the archetype slide is the brand's and sits right after the brand CVP.

Margaret Mark & Carol Pearson's twelve archetypes. A brand gets **two**: the leading one carries its essence; the complementary one covers the leading one's risks for this particular brand and market.

## The wheel

Two axes, four quadrants, three archetypes each:

| Quadrant | Archetypes |
|---|---|
| Independence (self-fulfilment) | Innocent / Ребёнок, Explorer / Искатель, Sage / Мудрец |
| Mastery (risk, leave a mark) | Hero / Герой, Outlaw / Бунтарь, Magician / Маг |
| Belonging (connection) | Regular Guy / Славный малый, Lover / Любовник, Jester / Шут |
| Stability (order, control) | Caregiver / Заботливый, Creator / Творец, Ruler / Правитель |

Axes: freedom ↔ order (Свобода ↔ Порядок), independence ↔ belonging (Независимость ↔ Принадлежность).

## Cards

| Archetype | Desire | Fear | Shadow / risk | Example brands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Innocent | Happiness, purity | Doing wrong, punishment | Naive, sugary, unbelievable | Dove, Coca-Cola (early), Evian |
| Explorer | Freedom to find yourself by exploring the world | Being trapped, conforming, emptiness | Egoism, recklessness, unreliable partner, breaking traditions | Jeep, The North Face, Red Bull, GoPro, Land Rover, Starbucks |
| Sage | Truth, understanding | Being misled, ignorance | Cold, lecturing, slow | Google, BBC, McKinsey |
| Hero | Prove worth through courageous action | Weakness, giving up | Arrogance, aggression, needs an enemy | Nike, FedEx, US Army |
| Outlaw | Revolution, break what doesn't work | Powerlessness, irrelevance | Destructive, alienating, illegal edge | Harley-Davidson, Diesel, Virgin |
| Magician | Transform reality, make dreams happen | Unintended consequences | Manipulative, overpromising | Disney, Apple (partly), Dyson |
| Regular Guy | Connection, belonging | Standing out, being rejected | Bland, no voice, lost self | IKEA, Visa, Snickers, Wrangler, GAP |
| Lover | Intimacy, pleasure | Being unwanted, alone | Vain, superficial, exclusionary | Chanel, Godiva, Victoria's Secret |
| Jester | Live in the moment, enjoy | Boredom | Frivolous, not taken seriously | M&M's, Old Spice, Skittles |
| Caregiver | Protect and help others | Selfishness, ingratitude | Martyr, smothering, guilt-tripping | Johnson & Johnson, Volvo, Nivea |
| Creator | Create something of lasting value | Mediocrity | Perfectionism, self-indulgence | LEGO, Adobe, Crayola |
| Ruler | Control, order, stability | Chaos, being overthrown | Authoritarian, cold, distant | Mercedes-Benz, Rolex, Microsoft |

## Method

1. **Leading archetype.** Name it and say why it fits — from the Why, the ladder, the principles, the category. Give the card: desire, goal, fear, strategy, reward, and the brand's line in that archetype's voice (*a national car maker as Explorer: we find a new angle on familiar things and open opportunities others don't see*).
2. **The client's archetype.** Which part of the customer the brand appeals to, and the cultural reason (*a car maker in a country with a nomadic past: even people who want stability, belonging and mastery have a dormant freedom-loving part — "I want to move freely toward my goals so my life gets better and fuller"*). Then **the brand's role**: *paves the way and inspires to move toward the goal*.
3. **Risks.** Name the leading archetype's shadow sides **for this brand in this market**, specifically (Explorer for that car maker in a traditional society: individualism vs care for others; adventurism vs a reliable partner; iconoclasm vs respect for elders and family).
4. **Complementary archetype.** Chosen from the risks: the one whose strengths cancel them. Give its card, why it fits the business (everyday use, fair price, better than the elite alternative), and what it does for the leading one point by point. Then the brand's line in its voice.

## Checks

- The complementary archetype answers named risks, not a wish for warmth.
- The pair doesn't contradict itself on the wheel's same axis without a reason stated.
- Both lines sound like the brand could say them in an ad.
