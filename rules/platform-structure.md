# Full brand platform

The order of the finished platform. Build in the working order, present in the presentation order.

## Working order

1. Inputs loaded, questions asked and answered.
2. Get-To-By brief with the insight (`brief-get-to-by.md`, `insight.md`) — what the platform has to solve.
3. Value proposition and message ladder together, each checking the other (`value-proposition.md`, `message-ladder.md`). Fix breaks before going on.
4. Archetype pair (`archetypes.md`).
5. Principles, tone, visual principle (`principles-tone-visual.md`).
6. Agree the whole text in the chat; save to the project.
7. Ask the user where to build the deck, then build it in the brand's style.

## Presentation order — a product, channel or launch inside a brand

The order when the work is about one division's product or news rather than the whole brand.

**Four sections, each opened by a divider slide.** Russian decks: «О бренде», «О продукте», «Задача», «Полезное для брифа».

- **About the brand** — describes what exists: the brand, the division, the brand CVP, the brand archetype.
- **About the product** — describes the product and how it speaks: the news, the product CVP, the ladder that checks it, principles, tone of voice, visual principle.
- **The task** — what we are asked to do and what we propose: the brief, the audience, the insight, the tensions, the key messages.
- **Useful for the brief** — reference the creative team will need while working on the brief: the brand's mission and vision (the brand's own, and ours marked "proposed" if we wrote them), the key points of the brand book (colour, type, signature devices, what the brand never does), the visual language (photo / illustration modes, casting rules, who is the hero). Taken from the client's brand book or the captured brand pack; each slide titled "Useful for the brief: …". Mission and vision live here, not in the brand section.
- **Appendix** — after the closing slide: open questions and working material.

The first two sections only describe. Nothing about the task goes there — not even a closing line on the news slide; the task has its own section.

**Every slide title says plainly what the slide is:** "About the brand: [name]", "About the division", "Brand Value Proposition", "Brand CVP: Why, How, What", "Brand archetype", "Product CVP", "Brief", "Brief: insight", "Message ladder", "Tone of voice". Statement slides get such a title too, above the statement. No clever headings in place of the label.

1. **The whole brand.** What it is and where: a few key markets / regions, or "worldwide". Even when the work is for one division, start with the parent brand. Origin facts are welcome when the brand uses them itself (Apple is happy to say it's from Cupertino), and left out when the brand avoids them — e.g. international companies with Russian roots now rarely mention where they were founded. Check how the brand describes itself before naming its origin.
2. **The division.** What the division under discussion does and where (a ride-hailing brand → its delivery division in one region).
3. **Brand CVP as text** — the "We believe…" statement on its own slide.
4. **Brand CVP by points** — Why / How / What.
5. **Brand archetype** — the archetype is the brand's. A product rarely has one; if a product has its own archetype, it is a sub-brand and gets its own platform.
6. **The news, briefly** — what is new: a launch of a new activity, a new product, an event. Description only.
7. **Product CVP** — text first, then by points, then the per-audience table.
8. **Message ladder** and its verdict.
9. **Principles, tone of voice, visual principle** — how the product speaks.
10. **Brief** — the Get-To-By slide is titled **Brief** (in Russian decks «Бриф»), opening the task section, followed by the audience, the insight and the alternative tensions.
11. **Key messages.**
12. **Useful for the brief** — mission and vision, brand book key points, visual language. Then the closing slide.
13. **Appendix** — a divider after the closing slide; open questions and any working material (hypotheses to confirm, alternatives, sources) go here, never in the main flow.

### Slide conventions

- **About slides open with the name.** The first words of the body are the brand or division name, highlighted: "**[Brand]** is a global …", "**[Division]** is the brand's … service".
- **CVP by points is a two-column layout,** not a body list: a narrow column of labels (Why / How / What, each highlighted) and a wide column with one sentence per label, aligned row by row.
- **Ladder layout:** the steps climb from bottom left to top right; the "It includes" box sits under the upper steps with an arrow from the step it proves; the entry points box sits top left.

## Presentation order — a full brand platform

1. **Brand descriptor** — one line: what the brand is, plainly, as a category. *Nike — a global sportswear company.* When the work is about a product, a second line for the product. Every comstrat document opens with it, a brief included.
2. **Vision** — from the Why.
3. **Mission** — "We don't just X. We Y, so that Z."
4. **Principles.**
5. **Value proposition** — brand CVP, then product CVP: each with the "We believe…" statement, the circle, the per-audience table and sentences (Master, B2C, B2B, HR, GR as relevant).
6. **Message ladder** — with the "it includes" box and audience entry points.
7. **Ecosystem / product architecture** — only if the brand has one (e.g. a car maker's ecosystem of financing, service and loyalty modules per audience + shared tools). Not built by this skill unless asked; give it a place.
8. **Archetype** — leading (why, client's archetype, brand's role, characteristics and risks), complementary (card, why, what it fixes, brand's line).
9. **Tone of voice** — rules with ✔/✖.
10. **Visual principle.**

A brief-only job stops at step 2 of the working order; a "check our platform" job runs the tests from each rule file and returns a list of breaks with fixes.
