# Value proposition — Why, How, What

Simon Sinek's Golden Circle, adapted. The proposition is a story about the brand, not about a feature: a statement that resonates with the audience emotionally and strategically and holds for all or most of the brand's products.

## Two levels: brand and product

Always write the CVP twice:

- **Brand CVP** — the brand as a whole (or the business unit the work is for, e.g. a company's delivery division). Holds for all or most products; never names one.
- **Product CVP** — the specific product, service or channel the task is about. It may say what the product gives, but it leads with the emotional How and its Why **is the brand's Why** (or a direct step toward it). A product Why that doesn't climb into the brand Why is a break — the message ladder shows it.

Both levels get the rings, the per-audience table (where audiences differ) and the belief statement below.

## Belief statement

Each CVP is also written as one short statement in the Sinek form, the version that goes on a slide and that the client can say out loud:

> **We believe** [the Why as a conviction about the world]. **That's why** [the How — what we do for the person, emotionally]. **We do it with / by** [the What — the proof].

The client's language (Russian: «Мы верим, что… Поэтому мы… Для этого мы…»). Equivalent openings are fine when "we believe" sounds forced for the brand: "Every … deserves …", "We're here so that …". Three sentences at most; if it needs more, the rings aren't sharp yet.

## The three rings

**Why — the big goal.** Why the brand exists beyond making money. One for the whole brand, usually phrased for the person: *so that everyone can move freely and confidently toward their goals in their own country* (a national car maker). It can widen for some audiences (GR adds *so that the economy grows and the country becomes the best place to live and work*).

**How — the emotional benefit.** It must answer the audience's emotional need, not list capabilities. The car maker, B2C: *simplicity, convenience and accessibility of buying and owning a car*. HR: *stability, confidence in tomorrow, room to grow*. B2B: *understanding, support and benefit*.

**What — the rational proof.** The concrete things that make the How true: products, tools, programs, the ecosystem. Joined to How with "благодаря / thanks to". The What never leads the proposition.

Where the brand's unique approach matters (Sinek's original How — Nike's breakthrough innovation), it goes into What as the proof, or into the mission sentence; the How ring stays emotional.

## Points in the brand's voice

When the CVP is laid out by points (Why / How / What), each point is a full sentence, not a noun phrase — in the first person ("We help…", "We offer…") or the third ("The brand offers…", "[Brand] creates…"); one voice across all three points. First person: *Why — We help small businesses get a fair chance next to the big ones. How — We're on the side of the people who do everything alone. What — We offer delivery at a price that's fair to both sides…* 

## Per audience

One Why, several How/What pairs. Typical set: **Master** (everyone), **B2C**, **B2B**, **HR**, **GR**. Lay them out as a table (audience × What / How emotional / How rational / Why) and as one sentence each. The order of the sentence parts may change by audience — B2C can open with the benefit, Master with what we build.

Sentence pattern: *We [build / do — What, brand level], so that [Why]* — then per audience *[How emotional] thanks to [What rational], so that [Why for this audience].*

## Vision and mission

They come from the proposition, not as separate work:

- **Vision** = the Why as a statement about the world: *We remove barriers so that everyone can move freely and confidently toward their goals in their own country.*
- **Mission** = "We don't just X. We Y, so that Z": the What at brand level + the Why. *We don't just make and sell cars. We create a new kind of car industry in our country and develop a mobility ecosystem, so that movement becomes more accessible and convenient for everyone.*

## Tests

- **Both levels present.** Brand CVP and product CVP, each with a belief statement; the product's Why climbs into the brand's.
- **Brand, not feature.** For the brand CVP: would the statement survive the brand launching a new product line? If it names a feature, a spec or one product, flag it.
- **Competitor swap.** Put the main competitor's name in. If it still reads true, the Why or How is too generic.
- **Emotional need.** Name the need the How answers. If you can't, it's a rational claim in the wrong ring.
- **Ladder check.** Run the message ladder against it (`message-ladder.md`). A break in the ladder means the circle probably doesn't work — say so.

## Reference

Useful public examples to show clients, each read as What / How / Why: Nike (*expand human potential — through breakthrough sport innovation, more sustainable products, a diverse global team and local communities*), Mars Wrigley, Mars Food. The Nike one also maps How onto key competence + E and G of ESG.
