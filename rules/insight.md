# Insight

## Form

**"I want X, but Y."** Said in the audience's voice, about their life, not about the brand. X is a real desire or need; Y is what stands in the way. The tension between them is the insight; the brand later earns the right to resolve it.

> I want my own car and to move freely toward my goals, but buying and owning one here feels complicated, expensive and risky.

## Where the tension comes from

Look in both places, and say which one each insight comes from:

- **Product / category** — friction around using, buying, choosing, owning the thing.
- **Culture / society** — the social context around the product, category or brand: norms, shame, status, generational shifts, local identity, what people say vs do. Example: in a country with a nomadic past, even people who want stability have a dormant freedom-loving part.

## Tests

- **Nod test.** Someone from the audience who never heard of the brand would say "yes, that's me".
- **Has a but.** Without Y it is a wish; without X it is a complaint.
- **Not a fact.** A statistic or a market observation is a supporting fact, not an insight.
- **Not a benefit.** If the brand's name fits inside the sentence, it's a proposition wearing an insight's clothes.
- **Opens a door.** It points to something the brand can actually do or stand for.

## Delivery

Give one to three, each tagged product or cultural, each with the evidence from the inputs it rests on. If the inputs give nothing to build on, ask the user for audience material (research, reviews, interviews, their own observation) rather than inventing.
