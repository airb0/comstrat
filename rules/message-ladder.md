# Message ladder

The most important tool. A chain of "so that" steps climbing from what the company does with its hands to the biggest goal it serves. It shows that the big words at the top are earned by the facts at the bottom.

## Shape

Worked example: a national car maker. Five to six steps, bottom to top, each joined by **"чтобы / so that"**:

1. **What we do** — the plain operational fact. *We produce, distribute, sell and service cars.*
2. **What we create** — the brand-level thing that work adds up to. *We create a new kind of car industry and a mobility ecosystem around it.*
3. **For the person** — *so that people's most varied aspirations can come true.*
4. **For the person, sharper** — *so that everyone can reach their goals in their own country.*
5. **For the system** — *so that the country's economy grows.*
6. **For the world / the country** — *so that the country becomes the best place to live and work.*

Two attachments:

- **"It includes" box** — the proofs behind the lower steps: new plants, technology, consulting, partnerships (banks, service stations, fuel, buses, farm machinery…).
- **Audience entry points** — consumers, employees, partners, government each step on at a different rung. This is what lets one ladder produce several value propositions.

## Building it

Climb with "why? → so that". Then descend with "how? → by / thanks to": every step must be delivered by the step below it. Both directions have to read naturally.

## Break checks

Name the step and the reason when any of these happen:

- **Jump** — a step doesn't follow from the one below; something is missing.
- **Restatement** — a step says the same as the previous one in other words.
- **Unearned** — nothing in the "it includes" box supports the step.
- **Generic top** — the upper steps fit any brand in any category (swap test).
- **Money as a step** — "so that money stays in the neighbourhood", "so that sales grow". Money is only ever a means; the value is always something else. Either drop the step or add the one above it that answers "why does that matter?". The same goes for growth, profit and efficiency.
- **Feature at the bottom only** — the ladder never leaves the product; nothing for the person.

## Ladder ↔ circle

Either order works, and the other one checks it.

- **Ladder first:** bottom (1–2) → What, middle (2–4) → How and the emotional benefit, top (4–6) → Why.
- **Circle first:** lay the circle onto the steps and climb. Where the ladder breaks or the "so that" turns illogical, **warn: the circle most likely doesn't work either**, show the step, propose the fix.

## Output

The ladder as text (numbered steps, the box, the entry points), then a short verdict: holds / breaks at step N because …
