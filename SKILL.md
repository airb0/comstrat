# Comstrat

A communication strategy toolkit: what the brand stands for, who it talks to, what it says and how it sounds. The user is often the creative pair the brief is written for — the usual job is to take a pile of unclear client inputs and make the task readable.

## Where it sits

comstrat makes the task (the brief), the brand meaning (the platform) and the message system. It stops there: generating ideas, building the pitch around an idea, designing physical brand experiences and capturing the brand's look (logo, fonts, colours) are the next steps, done separately.

## The tools

Each runs on its own ("just the ladder", "pick an archetype") or together as a full platform. Read the rule file before running a tool.

| Tool | Rule file | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Get-To-By brief | `rules/brief-get-to-by.md` | Brief for the creative pair, with the gaps listed |
| Insight | `rules/insight.md` | One to three tensions "I want X, but Y" |
| Value proposition (Why-How-What) | `rules/value-proposition.md` | Brand CVP and product CVP, each with a "We believe…" statement and per-audience lines; vision and mission fall out of the brand level |
| Message ladder | `rules/message-ladder.md` | The "so that" chain with proofs and audience entry points; break report |
| Archetype | `rules/archetypes.md` | Leading + complementary, risks, the brand's role |
| Principles, tone, visual | `rules/principles-tone-visual.md` | 4–6 principles, tone do/don't pairs, one visual principle |
| Full platform | `rules/platform-structure.md` | The whole platform, in presentation order |

## How to work

1. **Load everything the client sent.** Brief, decks, transcripts, links, the site. Read light digests before heavy originals.
2. **Ask before inventing.** When the inputs are thin or contradict each other, list the questions and wait for answers. Do not fill holes with plausible hypotheses unannounced. Questions go in one batch, most decisive first, each saying why it matters.
3. **Open with the brand descriptor.** Every document starts with one line saying what the brand is as a category (*Nike — a global sportswear company*), plus a line for the product if the work is about one. Not a slogan, not the proposition.
4. **Content first, form later.** Agree the text in the chat and save it as a markdown document in the project (`<project>/comstrat/YYYY.MM.DD HHMM <name>.md`). The deck comes only after the content is agreed.
5. **Ask where the deck is built.** Usually Google Slides in the brand's style, but ask the user every time — don't assume a format or a tool.

## Language

Russian-speaking client → Russian. Anyone else → English. A Russian client sometimes gets English too — ask if unclear. Talk to the user in the user's own language.

## Non-negotiables

- **Brand, not feature.** The brand CVP must hold for all or most of the brand's products and resonate on an emotional, strategic level. A product feature as the proposition is a fail — flag it. The product CVP is written too, but it leads with emotion and climbs into the brand's Why.
- **The ladder checks the circle.** Whichever is built first, the other tests it. If a step of the ladder breaks or the "so that" stops being logical, say plainly that the circle most likely doesn't work either, and name the step.
- **Insight has a "but".** No tension, no insight — just a fact or a wish.
- **Archetype is the brand's.** Products don't get one; a product with its own archetype is a sub-brand.
- **Two archetypes, not one.** The complementary one exists to cover the leading one's named risks; pick it from those risks, not from taste.
